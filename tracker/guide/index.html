Quick Start

The Tracker builds real .bbbmod files — the same format the floating player on every page plays. Four channels, 32 rows, one pattern. Here's the fastest path from nothing to a sound you made yourself.

  1. Upload a sample. Click Upload Sample and pick a short WAV or MP3 — a drum hit, a vocal chop, a synth stab, anything. It becomes Instrument 1 in your song. Upload a few more if you want variety; each one gets its own number.
  2. Click a cell in the grid. The pattern grid is 32 rows (top to bottom) by 4 channels (left to right) — think of it as 32 beats you can place notes on, across 4 simultaneous "voices."
  3. Set the cell. Below the grid, pick a Note (like C4 or A3), an Instrument (the number from step 1), and a Volume (0–64), then click Set Cell. The grid cell updates to show what you picked.
  4. Repeat across a few rows and channels — that's your song taking shape. Arrow keys move the selected cell around if clicking is slow going.
  5. Hit Preview to hear it. Stop cuts it off. Tweak Tempo/Speed at the top of the page to speed up or slow down the whole thing.
  6. Download saves a real .bbbmod file to your computer — always available, no sign-in needed.
  7. Publish puts it in the floating player for every visitor to hear. This one needs an active $10+/mo Patreon membership — see Publishing below.
That's it. Everything past this point is for getting more out of the same tool, not new tools you need to learn first.

Advanced Guide

How a cell works

Each of the 32×4 grid cells holds three things you control — Note, Instrument, Volume — plus two reserved-for-later fields (effect slots) the editor always leaves blank for now. A cell showing ··· is empty: nothing plays there. An empty cell in the middle of a held note is fine — the instrument's own sample decides how long the sound actually rings out; the grid just controls when new notes start.

Notes, octaves, and "unison"

When you upload a sample, the Tracker doesn't know what pitch it "is" — there's no auto-detection. Whatever pitch you hear when you upload it is treated as C4 (called "unison" in tracker terms). Every other note you pick in the editor is that same sample sped up or slowed down to reach a different pitch — C5 plays it an octave faster, C3 an octave slower, and so on down to G2 and up to B6 (the dropdown's full range). This means a drum sample doesn't really have a "correct" note — C4 keeps its recorded pitch, anything else changes its character too (a snare pitched way down starts sounding like a tom).

Tempo and Speed — how timing actually works

These two numbers together set how long each row lasts, using the same formula the classic Amiga trackers used:

seconds per row = (Speed × 2.5) ÷ Tempo

Raising Tempo speeds everything up. Raising Speed (counterintuitively) slows things down — it's ticks-per-row, not a tempo dial, a classic tracker quirk carried over on purpose. If your song feels twice as fast or slow as you expected, check Speed first, not just Tempo.

A 4-channel arrangement pattern that works

Nothing forces you into this, but it's the shape used for the two official player tracks (Canon in D, Für Elise), and it's a reliable starting template:

ChannelRoleNotes
1DrumsCycle between a few percussive samples (kick/snare/hihat) across different rows for a beat.
2BassOne low note per bar, sparse — it doesn't need to retrigger every row to feel present.
3Lead / melodyYour main line — this is usually what a listener follows.
4Pad / harmonySparse, held notes underneath everything else — glue, not the focus.

Channels are generic — the player doesn't know or care which one is "drums." This table is just a starting habit, not a rule the format enforces.

Sample tips

  • Mono only. Stereo uploads get downmixed to a single channel automatically (the editor just takes the left channel) — if stereo width matters to your sample, that detail is lost.
  • Short samples read as "hits," long samples read as "held notes." A quarter-second drum hit and a 2-second pad behave very differently once pitched and retriggered across a pattern — if something sounds muddy, try a shorter sample first.
  • Trim silence before uploading. The editor doesn't trim for you — a sample with a half-second of silence at the start will sound "late" every time it triggers.
  • Volume is 0–64, not 0–100 or 0–1. That's the classic tracker scale — 64 is full volume for that cell, not a percentage.

Publishing

Preview and Download work for anyone, signed in or not. Publish is different — it puts your track in the floating player that plays on every page of the site, for every visitor, credited with your Patreon display name. Because that's a public act rather than private storage, it's gated to an active $10+/mo Patreon membership — the same threshold used for personal file storage (ROM Storage) elsewhere on the site.

If you're signed in but not at that tier, the Publish button stays visible but disabled with a message explaining why. If you're not signed in at all, you'll see a link to sign in with Patreon first.

Once published, your track appears under Your Published Mods further down this page, with an Unpublish button — you're always in control of taking something back down. See the Terms of Service for the full policy on published content.

Current limitations

Worth knowing so you're not hunting for a setting that isn't there yet:

  • 32 rows, 1 pattern, fixed. No pattern chaining or song-length beyond one 32-row loop yet.
  • No loop points. The underlying format supports sample loop start/length, but the editor doesn't expose a way to set them — every uploaded sample plays once through per trigger.
  • No effect commands. The format reserves space for them (volume slides, portamento, etc.) but the editor always leaves that field empty. Notes, instrument, and volume are your full toolkit for now.
  • No undo. Clearing or overwriting a cell is immediate — there's no history to step back through yet.
  • Requires a browser with Web Audio API support (every modern browser has this; nothing to install).
